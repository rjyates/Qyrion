# Qyrion Business Strengtheners

## Purpose

This document captures ideas that make Qyrion stronger as a business, harder to copy, and more valuable to customers.

## Strategic Insight

Qyrion should not only sell scanning.

Scanning is a feature.

Qyrion should sell readiness:

- visibility
- planning
- education
- private AI
- evidence
- governance
- ongoing monitoring

## 1. Qyrion Readiness Score

Create a clear score that helps companies understand their post-quantum preparedness.

Inputs:

- Number of quantum-vulnerable cryptographic components
- Asset exposure
- Data sensitivity
- Data confidentiality lifetime
- Business criticality
- Vendor dependency
- Migration complexity
- Existing crypto-agility

Why it helps:

Scores are easy for executives to understand and track over time.

Possible product phrase:

Know your score before quantum changes the rules.

## 2. Quantum Exposure Clock

Create a visual tool that shows how urgent a system is based on how long its data must stay confidential.

Example:

- Public marketing site: low urgency
- Customer payment records: higher urgency
- Medical records: high urgency
- Legal or defense data: very high urgency

Why it helps:

It makes "harvest now, decrypt later" understandable without requiring deep cryptography knowledge.

## 3. CBOM Diffing

Let customers compare CBOMs over time.

Examples:

- Did this software release add new quantum-vulnerable cryptography?
- Did a vendor improve or worsen their cryptographic posture?
- Did remediation reduce risk?
- Did a certificate renewal change key type or signature algorithm?

Why it helps:

It turns CBOM from a one-time report into an ongoing monitoring product.

## 4. Qyrion Evidence Pack

Generate simple evidence packages for:

- leadership
- auditors
- customers
- insurers
- procurement teams
- board updates

Contents:

- Executive summary
- CBOM summary
- risk score
- top findings
- remediation roadmap
- data privacy statement
- change history

Why it helps:

Customers often need proof that they are preparing, not just internal findings.

## 5. Vendor Quantum-Risk Portal

Help companies understand whether vendors are ready for post-quantum migration.

Features:

- vendor questionnaire
- vendor CBOM upload
- readiness score
- AI summary of vendor answers
- renewal/procurement risk notes

Why it helps:

Most companies do not control all of their cryptography. Vendor risk will matter.

## 6. Qyrion Academy

Create a small education hub for business and security teams.

Content:

- short videos
- plain-English guides
- checklists
- industry pages
- glossary
- sample reports
- readiness quiz

Why it helps:

Education creates trust, leads, and authority. It also supports the "have a plan before you need one" message.

## 7. Private AI Trust Receipt

Every Qyrion report should include a trust receipt that explains what data was used and what data did not leave.

Example:

This report was generated from public TLS metadata. No private keys, source code, logs, customer data, secrets, or internal infrastructure maps were collected.

Why it helps:

It makes the privacy promise visible and verifiable.

## 8. Standards-Aware CBOM Export

Qyrion should track existing CBOM standards instead of pretending the market starts from zero.

Near-term direction:

- Keep Qyrion's internal schema simple.
- Study CycloneDX CBOM alignment.
- Consider exporting CycloneDX-compatible CBOM files later.
- Keep Qyrion's scoring, education, and private AI workflow proprietary.

Why it helps:

Standards compatibility makes Qyrion easier to adopt while preserving a proprietary layer.

## 9. Industry-Specific Readiness Packs

Create tailored readiness packs for specific markets.

First candidates:

- healthcare
- financial services
- law firms
- defense suppliers
- SaaS companies
- critical infrastructure

Each pack should include:

- common risks
- plain-English buyer explanation
- checklist
- sample CBOM
- suggested roadmap

Why it helps:

Generic quantum security is abstract. Industry-specific planning feels concrete.

## 10. Qyrion Policy Engine

Let customers define rules.

Examples:

- Flag RSA under 3072 bits.
- Flag all ECC certificates for PQC tracking.
- Flag systems protecting data for more than 7 years.
- Flag vendors without a PQC migration statement.
- Flag production systems missing ownership metadata.

Why it helps:

Policy turns Qyrion into a governance product, not just a scanner.

## 11. Free Public CBOM Scan

Offer a small website tool:

Enter a public domain and receive a basic public TLS CBOM.

Rules:

- Only public metadata.
- No invasive scanning.
- Clear privacy explanation.
- Use it as lead generation.

Why it helps:

It demonstrates the product and teaches the market at the same time.

## 12. Readiness Roadmap Generator

After producing a CBOM, Qyrion should generate a plan:

- what to monitor
- what to migrate first
- who should own each action
- what can wait
- what vendors to contact
- what evidence to collect

Why it helps:

The business message is "have a plan before you need one." The product should literally create that plan.
