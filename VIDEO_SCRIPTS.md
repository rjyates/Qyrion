# Qyrion Educational Video Scripts

## Video Style

Keep every video simple, calm, and practical.

Target length:

60 to 120 seconds.

Voice:

Clear, reassuring, and business-friendly.

Rule:

Teach one concept per video.

## Video 1: What Is Quantum Cybersecurity?

### Goal

Explain why quantum computing matters to cybersecurity without making it sound like an immediate disaster.

### Script Draft

Most of today's digital security depends on cryptography.

Cryptography helps protect websites, banking systems, medical records, software updates, business messages, and private data.

Some of the most common cryptography used today, including RSA and elliptic curve cryptography, could eventually be broken by a powerful enough quantum computer.

That does not mean everything needs to be replaced overnight.

It means businesses need to understand where they use cryptography, which systems may become vulnerable, and what they should prepare to change.

Quantum cybersecurity is about making that plan before the pressure arrives.

At Qyrion, we help organizations start with visibility: what cryptography they use, where it lives, and what should be prioritized for post-quantum readiness.

The first step is not panic.

The first step is knowing what you have.

### Takeaway

Quantum cybersecurity starts with visibility, not panic.

## Video 2: What Is Harvest Now, Decrypt Later?

### Goal

Explain why quantum risk matters before quantum attacks are common.

### Script Draft

Some encrypted data only needs to stay private for a few days.

Other data needs to stay private for years or decades.

That difference matters.

Harvest now, decrypt later is the risk that an attacker could steal encrypted data today and wait until future technology makes it easier to decrypt.

This is especially important for businesses that handle medical records, financial data, legal documents, intellectual property, government information, or long-term customer records.

The risk is not that every system is being broken by quantum computers today.

The risk is that some data being protected today may still be valuable when quantum capabilities improve.

That is why quantum readiness starts early.

Qyrion helps organizations identify which systems protect long-lived sensitive data, so they can plan migration before it becomes urgent.

### Takeaway

If data must stay private for years, quantum planning should start now.

## Video 3: What Is A CBOM?

### Goal

Introduce the Cryptographic Bill of Materials as Qyrion's core concept.

### Script Draft

Many organizations use a Software Bill of Materials, or SBOM, to understand what software components they depend on.

A CBOM is similar, but for cryptography.

CBOM stands for Cryptographic Bill of Materials.

It helps answer questions like:

What encryption do we use?

Where do we use RSA or elliptic curve cryptography?

Which certificates, protocols, libraries, and signing systems do we depend on?

Which parts may need attention as post-quantum standards become more widely adopted?

A company cannot migrate cryptography it cannot see.

That is why Qyrion starts with a CBOM.

It gives businesses a practical map of their cryptographic exposure, without requiring sensitive data to be sent to external AI systems.

### Takeaway

A CBOM helps you see your cryptography before you need to change it.
