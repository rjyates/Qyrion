What quantum security means

Much of today's digital security depends on public-key cryptography, including RSA and elliptic curve cryptography. These systems help protect websites, software updates, identity systems, business communications, and sensitive records.

A powerful enough quantum computer could eventually break many of these public-key systems. That does not mean every business needs to replace everything today. It means businesses need visibility and a plan.

What harvest now, decrypt later means

Some data only needs to stay private for a short time. Other data must stay private for years or decades. Harvest-now-decrypt-later is the risk that encrypted data could be stolen today and decrypted later if quantum capabilities improve.

This matters most for organizations with long-lived sensitive data: healthcare, finance, legal, government, defense, research, and intellectual property-heavy businesses.

Why a CBOM is the first step

A Cryptographic Bill of Materials, or CBOM, helps a company understand what cryptography it uses and where that cryptography lives. A company cannot migrate cryptography it cannot see.

Qyrion starts with CBOMs because inventory comes before migration. The goal is to turn unknown cryptographic exposure into a practical readiness plan.

What to do first

  1. Identify public endpoints and certificates.
  2. Create a small CBOM for those assets.
  3. Record which systems protect long-lived sensitive data.
  4. Assign ownership for cryptographic migration planning.
  5. Track changes over time instead of waiting for an emergency.
