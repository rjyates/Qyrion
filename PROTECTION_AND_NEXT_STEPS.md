# Qyrion Protection And Next Steps

## Important Note

This is a founder planning document, not legal advice. For trademark filings, patent strategy, contracts, incorporation, taxes, and investment documents, use a qualified attorney or accountant when you are ready.

## Protection Strategy

The goal is not to make Qyrion impossible to copy. No early-stage idea can be fully protected by secrecy alone.

The goal is to make Qyrion hard to copy by protecting the name, controlling disclosure, building proof quickly, and creating a product direction that compounds.

## What To Protect

### 1. Brand

Protect:

- Qyrion
- Qyrion BlackBox
- Qyrion CBOM
- Any logo or visual identity
- Tagline, if it becomes central

Why it matters:

The brand is often easier to protect early than the general business idea.

Immediate actions:

- Search for obvious name conflicts.
- Secure domain names if available.
- Secure social handles.
- Use Qyrion consistently in public materials.
- Consider a federal trademark application when the brand direction is stable.

### 2. Confidential Business Details

Protect:

- Product architecture
- Scanner logic
- CBOM schema
- Private AI workflow
- Customer discovery notes
- Roadmap
- Pricing strategy
- Buyer lists
- Technical diagrams

Immediate actions:

- Keep sensitive files in a private repository.
- Do not post detailed architecture publicly.
- Use NDAs for serious partner, contractor, or investor discussions when appropriate.
- Share the high-level vision publicly, not the implementation details.

### 3. Technical Inventions

Potentially protect:

- A unique CBOM generation method
- A private AI risk-scoring workflow
- A cryptographic metadata sanitization process
- A remediation governance system
- A local/air-gapped quantum-readiness engine

Immediate actions:

- Keep an invention log.
- Date each major technical idea.
- Save diagrams, prototypes, and design notes.
- Avoid public technical disclosure before talking to a patent attorney if the invention seems novel.

### 4. Copyrighted Materials

Protect:

- Website copy
- Reports
- Product documentation
- UI designs
- Training materials
- Code

Immediate actions:

- Keep source files.
- Keep authorship records.
- Use copyright notices on public-facing materials.
- Register copyright later for important finished assets if needed.

### 5. Execution Moat

Protect through speed and focus:

- Build the first prototype.
- Create the first CBOM schema.
- Produce sample reports.
- Collect customer feedback.
- Improve faster than copycats.

Ideas get copied. Operational learning is harder to copy.

## Qyrion's Defensible Wedge

Qyrion should own this narrow category:

Private AI-generated CBOMs for post-quantum readiness.

This is more defensible than "quantum cybersecurity" because it is specific, technical, and tied to a real future need.

## Safe Public Description

Use this when talking publicly:

Qyrion helps organizations create private Cryptographic Bills of Materials so they can understand and prepare for post-quantum cybersecurity risk without exposing sensitive systems or data to external AI.

## Private Description

Keep this internal:

Qyrion BlackBox Lite will generate local CBOMs from public TLS/certificate data first, then expand into repositories, dependencies, cloud assets, vendor risk, and private AI-generated migration planning.

## First Legal/Business Setup Steps

For a U.S.-based founder, the usual early sequence is:

1. Choose business structure.
2. Register the business if you are ready to operate formally.
3. Get an EIN if needed.
4. Open a business bank account.
5. Start basic bookkeeping.
6. Consider trademark filing for Qyrion.
7. Talk to an attorney before patent-related public disclosure.

Do not let this become a blocker. The first practical milestone is still the prototype and brand validation.

## Anti-Copying Rules

- Do not publish the full CBOM schema until we decide whether it should be open, closed, or partially open.
- Do not publish detailed scanner internals.
- Do not show private roadmap details in public pitch materials.
- Do not invite contractors into the repo without written terms.
- Do not pitch implementation details casually.
- Do not rely on secrecy alone.

## New Ideas

- Create a "Qyrion CBOM Format" that can become a standard, but keep advanced scoring private.
- Offer a free public-domain CBOM scan, then keep private scans self-hosted.
- Build a local-only "trust receipt" showing what data never left the customer's environment.
- Add CBOM diffing so companies can see whether a new release increased quantum risk.
- Create a "Quantum Exposure Clock" showing how data lifetime and Q-day assumptions affect urgency.
